# Public-domain English translations

US rule: works **published before 1930** are public domain regardless of
translator. All editions below clear that bar. Quote freely; credit the
translator as a courtesy.

Browse the full collection at the Internet Sacred Text Archive:
https://sacred-texts.com/hin/index.htm

| Text | Translator | Published | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mahabharata (complete, 18 books) | Kisari Mohan Ganguli | 1883–1896 | The only complete PD English Mahabharata. Full text: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm |
| Ramayana | Ralph T. H. Griffith | 1870–1874 | First complete PD English Ramayana |
| Ramayana & Mahabharata (abridged) | Romesh Chunder Dutt | 1899 | Readable condensed version; good for katha summaries |
| Bhagavad Gita ("The Song Celestial") | Edwin Arnold | 1885 | Poetic; pairs well with a literal translation for study pages |
| Hymns of the Rig Veda | Ralph T. H. Griffith | 1889–1892 | For mantra source verses |
| Upanishads (Sacred Books of the East) | F. Max Müller | 1879–1884 | Principal Upanishads |
| Laws of Manu | Georg Bühler | 1886 | Dharmashastra reference |

## What NOT to use

- **Gita Press Hindi/English translations** — feel traditional, but are 20th-century copyrighted works.
- **Prabhupada's "Bhagavad-gita As It Is"**, Easwaran, Mitchell, or any living/recent translator.
- **Modern retellings** (Devdutt Pattanaik, Amish, etc.) — copyrighted, no exceptions.
- Anyone's "108 mantras with meanings" book or blog post wholesale — the *selection, arrangement, and commentary* can be copyrighted even when the verses are ancient. Write your own.
