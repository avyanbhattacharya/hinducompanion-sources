# Audio — the highest-risk category

Recommendation: **do not host bhajan/mantra audio at launch.** Audio has
*two* stacked copyrights — the lyrics/composition **and** the specific
recording (performer + label rights). A traditional aarti with unknown
authorship can still be infringing as a *recording* if you lift someone's
rendition.

## Lyrics as text

- Traditional aartis/stotras of unknown authorship (e.g. classic
  "Om Jai Jagdish Hare"-type texts): generally safe **as text you type
  yourself**. Do not copy-paste from a lyrics site — their transcription
  and formatting choices are theirs.
- Film bhajans, modern kirtan compositions, anything by a living artist:
  copyrighted. Skip.

## If you add audio later

- Record your own (or commission) renditions of traditional texts, with
  written performer agreements.
- The Vedic Heritage Portal (https://vedicheritage.gov.in/) hosts
  government-recorded Vedic chanting — check its terms before embedding;
  government of India content is not automatically public domain.
- Never rip from YouTube "non-copyright bhajan" compilations — that label
  is meaningless and the uploader usually doesn't own the recording either.
