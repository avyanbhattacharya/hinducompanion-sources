# Sanskrit e-text archives

The verses themselves are millennia old and public domain. What varies is
each archive's terms for **its transcription/encoding**. Read before copying.

## Usable

### Sanskrit Wikisource — https://sa.wikisource.org/
- **License: CC BY-SA 4.0** (confirmed via the wiki's own API: `action=query&meta=siteinfo&siprop=rightsinfo`).
- Reuse, including commercial, is allowed **with attribution** (credit Sanskrit Wikisource, link the license: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).
- **ShareAlike propagates**: text derived from Wikisource must itself be shared under BY-SA. Record the page title, revision id (`oldid`), and fetch date per text so the exact revision is traceable — wiki pages change.
- Best for: Vedic samhitas and well-proofread core texts.

## Reference only — do not republish from these

### GRETIL — https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/
- Göttingen's academic e-text collection (often IAST romanization, not Devanagari).
- No reuse license is granted. Use for **cross-checking readings**, not as a source to copy.

### ⚠️ sanskritdocuments.org — the trap
- Huge, high-quality collection of stotras and mantras — and **explicitly not free to reuse**.
- Their footer: *"The file is not to be copied or reposted for promotion of any website or individuals or for commercial purpose without permission."*
- Their FAQ bars use *"to promote your own web-site, commercial or not, to get more traffic for your site."*
- **Do not copy text from sanskritdocuments.org onto hinducompanion.com.** Link to it as a reference at most, or ask permission per text.
- This is exactly the kind of site that *feels* public domain and isn't.
