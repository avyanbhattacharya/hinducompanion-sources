# Deity imagery — where to get it legally

## Primary source: Wikimedia Commons

Start here: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Hindu_deities
(670+ files across 41 subcategories, plus per-deity categories.)

**Check every image's license tag individually.** You will see three kinds:

| Tag | Meaning | Usable? |
|---|---|---|
| Public Domain (PD-art, PD-old) | Ancient sculpture, miniature paintings, pre-1930 prints | Yes, freely |
| CC BY / CC BY-SA | Modern photos of temples/idols, user uploads | Yes, with attribution (and ShareAlike if BY-SA) |
| Fair use / non-commercial | Rare on Commons, but verify | No — skip |

Good PD hunting grounds on Commons: Raja Ravi Varma paintings (d. 1906),
19th-century Company School paintings, archaeological sculpture photos where
the *photo itself* is PD or freely licensed.

## Rules of thumb

- **Ancient art** (temple sculpture, old miniatures) → public domain.
- **Modern calendar/framing-shop art** → assume copyrighted. The style that
  dominates Indian bazaars is almost entirely 20th-century commercial art.
- **Stock photos** (Shutterstock, Adobe Stock deity renders) → licensed only;
  don't pull from Google Images.
- **AI-generated deity images** → copyright-clean to use, but a sensitivity
  risk: some users find AI depictions of deities offensive. If used, keep them
  clearly illustrative, never as objects of veneration, and expect some pushback.

## Safest long-term play

Commission a small set of original illustrations in one consistent style
(artist assigns full rights in writing). One-time cost, zero license
ambiguity, and a distinctive visual identity no competitor can copy.
